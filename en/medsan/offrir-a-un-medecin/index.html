What to give a doctor: an art gift chosen by speciality

I’m William San, and I paint Medsan in Orléans, France. Here is how to choose a work for a doctor or a medical student, by speciality and by occasion.

Medsan began with a remark. I was passionate about medicine, and the person I was with at the time, a medical student, could not find a single anatomical painting she thought beautiful. I put my work out on Instagram and it took off; I have not stopped since. Each work is about one organ and one specific condition, from ectopic heartbeats to endometriosis, in a Japanese mood of koi and Hokusai’s waves, kintsugi and Imari porcelain. A doctor recognises their own field at a glance, and that is what makes these works good gifts.

The safest starting point is the speciality. A cardiologist has five works to choose from, an ophthalmologist three, a spinal surgeon six; the guide by speciality is below. Then comes the occasion. In France it is the thesis defence; in Britain, graduation and the first post as an F1 doctor. The date is fixed, so order as soon as you know it. For a new practice, the work will face patients, and I would suggest a calm image such as Corallium or Aqua Mentis. For a retirement the gift is often collective: split twenty ways, an original comes to €130 each.

Price depends on size, not on the image. A signed, numbered art print, limited to 20 copies per size, costs €300 in 40 × 40 cm and €450 in 60 × 60 cm; Ayame’s Spine and Dragonomy, both portrait format, come in 40 × 60 cm at €400. A black floater frame adds €80 in 40 × 40 and 40 × 60 cm, and the work arrives ready to hang. The originals are four oils on canvas, 80 × 80 cm, one-off pieces at €2,600 with a certificate of authenticity. A work in stock ships within five working days, to mainland France and Europe.

Your questions

What is a good graduation gift for a new doctor?

A work about the speciality they are heading for: a heart for a future cardiologist, a spine for a future rheumatologist. The date of a graduation or a thesis defence does not move, so order as soon as you know it. A work in stock ships within five working days.

What can I give a medical student on a budget?

A 40 × 40 cm signed, numbered art print costs €300, or €380 in its black floater frame. Start from the organ they love or the speciality they want. Split between six friends from the same year, it comes to €50 each.

What can a department give a doctor who is leaving?

For a whole department, an 80 × 80 cm original at €2,600 is a one-off piece, signed, with its certificate of authenticity; between twenty colleagues, that is €130 each. For a smaller group, a 60 × 60 cm print at €450 reads from across a room, in an office or on a ward corridor.

Does the painting come framed?

The 40 × 40 and 40 × 60 cm sizes can be ordered in a black floater frame for an extra €80: the work floats inside a matte black frame, with no glass, and arrives ready to hang. The 60 × 60 cm size comes unframed.

Will it arrive in time?

A work in stock ships within five working days, to mainland France and Europe. Systolic Sea is painted to order, and its lead time is confirmed by email. If your date is close, write to me before ordering and I will tell you whether it can be done.

Should I give an original or a print?

An original is the only one in existence: an 80 × 80 cm oil on canvas, signed, at €2,600, with a certificate of authenticity. A print belongs to an edition of 20 per size, each copy signed and numbered by hand, from €300. Medsan prints are not posters: once the twentieth copy of a size is sold, there are no more.
