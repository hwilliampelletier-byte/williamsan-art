Atelier Joconde: a father, his two sons and three signatures in Orléans

By William San, on 15 October 2026

On the round sign above our stand it says “Atelier Joconde, Fine Art Canvas by Le Yack”. Le Yack is my father. Behind that sign there are three painters from the same family: him, my brother Neo Pablo, and me, signing as Popsan. Atelier Joconde is our studio at LAB’O in Orléans, our gallery at atelierjoconde.com, and the stand we put up together at art fairs.

Each of us paints our own work under our own name. None of us paints for the other two, and the online gallery sells only original works by its three artists.

Le Yack, my father

My father paints shadows and solitude, like a woman on the phone in a glass phone box in the rain, or a man leaning against a red convertible outside a petrol station in the middle of the desert. His canvases tell suspended scenes, just before or just after something happens, and it is up to the viewer to finish the story. At fairs you can spot them from across the hall by their light, which owes a great deal to Edward Hopper. In the opening photo, taken on our stand in Monaco, L’ombre courbe shows a woman in a white dress leaning against the curved front of a motel, beside an empty car park; just below, in Kelly, a driver in white gloves is out on the road at nightfall, the city lights behind her.

He also paints cars to order. You send him photos of yours and he turns it into a painting. That is the studio’s custom car painting. His work is on his artist page.

Neo Pablo, my brother

Neo Pablo paints the city at the hour when the façades light up, with golden cathedrals, café terraces and streets wet with rain. In one of his canvases, three passers-by walk away down a pink and yellow Paris boulevard while their shadows stretch towards us in bright blue. He has chosen to stay anonymous. In our photos he always wears the same white fox mask covered in coloured drips. His work is at neopablo.art.

Popsan, and Medsan

As for me, I sign twice. Popsan is Japanese women emerging from the night by the glow of a lantern, a moon or a shop window. Medsan is the art of medicine in a Japanese mood, with hearts, spines or a jaw caught in Hokusai’s wave, things you would actually want on your wall.

Medsan came first, from a remark by my partner back then, who was studying medicine, and Popsan came later, so I could take on other subjects.

In September we were at the art3f fair in Monaco, then in October at the one in Luxembourg, where I had my Popsan wall. In November it will be Brussels. On a stand for three, we take turns, and each of us presents the other two’s paintings as readily as our own.

In Monaco, I told a visitor what I think about fairs: we do not do it for the money, we do it to get known, because the money does not always come, and it rarely does. A fair is there so that strangers remember our three names.

If you are passing through Orléans, or if a painting stopped you at a fair, write to me: I answer myself.
