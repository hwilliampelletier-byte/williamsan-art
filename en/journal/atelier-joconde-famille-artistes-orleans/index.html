Atelier Joconde: a father, his two sons and three signatures in Orléans

By William San, on 15 October 2026

On the round sign above our stand it says “Atelier Joconde, Fine Art Canvas by Le Yack”. Le Yack is my father. Behind that sign there are three painters from the same family: him, my brother Neo Pablo, and me, signing as Popsan. Atelier Joconde is our studio at LAB’O in Orléans, our gallery at atelierjoconde.com, and the stand we put up together at art fairs.

Each of us paints our own work under our own name. None of us paints for the other two, and the gallery sells only the original paintings of its three artists.

Le Yack, my father

My father paints shadows and solitude, somewhere between old cinema and classical painting. His canvases tell suspended scenes, just before or just after something happens, and it is up to the viewer to finish the story. At fairs you can spot them from across the hall by their light, which owes a great deal to Edward Hopper. In the opening photo, behind us, a 1940s couple leans against a fighter plane; on the left, women in evening dresses and a feathered dancer have lunch on a girder high above the city.

He also paints cars to order. You send him photos of yours and he turns it into a painting: that is the studio’s custom car painting. His work is on his artist page.

Neo Pablo, my brother

Neo Pablo paints the city at the hour when the façades light up: golden cathedrals, café terraces, streets wet with rain. His colours go as far as they can go. He paints in acrylic, and he has chosen to stay anonymous: in our photos he always wears the same white fox mask covered in coloured drips, and when the mask is not on his face, we add it to the picture. His work is at neopablo.art.

Popsan, and Medsan

As for me, I sign twice. Popsan is women of Japan stepping out of the night, by the glow of a lantern, a moon or a shop window. Medsan is the art of medicine in a Japanese mood: hearts, spines, a jaw caught in Hokusai’s wave, things you would actually want on your wall.

Medsan came first. I was passionate about medicine, and the person I was with at the time was studying it; she could not find a single anatomical painting she found beautiful. I wanted to prove her wrong. I started posting on Instagram, it took off, and I have not stopped since. Popsan came later, so I could take on other things: I have a lot of ideas, and one signature could not hold them all. Both fit in one sentence: Popsan looks for light in the shadows, Medsan turns medicine into art.

In September we were at the art3f fair in Monaco, then in October at the one in Luxembourg, where I had my Popsan wall. In November it will be Brussels. On a stand for three, we take turns, and each of us presents the other two’s paintings as readily as our own.

In Monaco, I told a visitor what I think about fairs: we do not do it for the money, we do it to get known, because the money does not always come, and it rarely does. A fair is there so that strangers remember our three names.

If you are passing through Orléans, or if a painting stopped you at a fair, write to me: I answer myself.
