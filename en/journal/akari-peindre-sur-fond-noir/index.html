Painting on a black background: how Akari steps out of the night

By William San, on 12 October 2026

Akari means “glow” in Japanese. It is the name of the painting I sold in September at the art3f fair in Monaco, and it may be the easiest of my paintings to describe. A woman in profile, in a blue kimono, looks down at a paper lantern she holds in front of her. Around her there is nothing but the night, and the lantern alone lights her face.

That light is the only one in the picture. The background is black, from edge to edge.

What I owe to Georges de La Tour

This painting is a tribute to Georges de La Tour, a seventeenth-century painter from Lorraine (1593-1652). He would set a candle beside a figure and let the whole picture depend on that flame, down to the place where your eye comes to rest. In Saint Joseph the Carpenter, at the Louvre, the child shields the flame with his hand and the light shines through his fingers. In The Newborn, at the Musée des Beaux-Arts in Rennes, the candle is hidden, so the baby seems to light the room on its own.

I wanted to take up that way of building colour out of light, in a more modern style and in my own, which looks towards Japan. The candle became a paper lantern painted with flowers, and the figure a woman with combs and pins in her hair. The principle did not change. A single source of light, and whatever it does not reach stays in shadow.

What the lantern lights

On the stand, when I was filmed explaining the technique, it took me several takes to find a clear sentence. That sentence stays in the studio, because my methods are my own.

What you can see on the canvas, though, can be told. The lantern is the brightest thing in the picture. Everything else is in shadow, and the lantern alone decides what deserves the light. In the detail below, the cheek and the bridge of the nose face the lantern. The ear and the nape, which cannot see it, stay almost black.

Where Akari is now

The canvas measures 60 × 60 cm. It sold during the fair, at the Chapiteau de Fontvieille, between 11 and 13 September 2026, and now belongs to a private collection. It was my first exhibition in Monaco.

In Monaco, whenever I presented Akari, I translated its name like this: the glow, the one you carry inside you.

The same night returns in other Popsan paintings. Tsuki, shown at the same fair, holds a moon in her hands instead of a lantern, in the same chiaroscuro, with a single light. It found a buyer in October, at the art3f fair in Luxembourg.

Others are still available. On the left of the Monaco Info photo, Le passage de la nuit shows a woman under an umbrella crossing a zebra crossing at night, in front of the lit window of a konbini. That is the painting I presented to Monaco Info that day. It too measures 60 × 60 cm, and on the stand I described it like this: the passage between a before and an after, like a doorway.

At the right-hand edge of the same photo is Shūden, also available, whose name means “last train” in Japanese. A lone man waits on a platform at night, at the edge of the yellow line. I wanted him to look a little like everyone: he could look like you, he could look like me.
